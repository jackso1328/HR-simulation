What Is "Human Resources"?

  • A resource = anything an org uses to create value (money, machines, data, technology)
  • Human Resources = people + their knowledge, skills, experience, and decision-making ability
  • Not just names in a spreadsheet — it's capability

Resources Need People to Use Them

  • Best technology ≠ results, without people who know how to use it
  • Example: unlimited computing power given to students with no training in programming/analytics — useless on its own
  • Same logic applies to organizations

Business Requirement → People Requirement

  • Business decision: "We want to grow this business"
  • Immediate people question: What roles? How many? What skills?
  • Key line: HR connects the organization's business requirements with its people and capabilities

What Is Human Resource Planning?

  • Determines: how many employees, what capabilities, and when they're needed
  • "Planning before hiring"
  • Not just "we need more people" — needs structured analysis

Case: DataTech's Expansion

  • DataTech: 1,000 employees → expanding BI business next year
  • Forecasted need: 1,300 employees
  • 100 moved internally + 50 trained internally = only 150 need external hiring

The Workforce Gap Formula

  • Reverse case: 900 needed, 1,000 available = 100 surplus → redeployment, not layoffs
Expected requirement: 0
Available/suitable: - 0
Gap (Shortage): 150

Quantity Isn't Enough

  • "500 employees" tells us nothing without knowing the type
  • 100 data analysts hired without SQL/statistics skills = skills gap
  • Two fixes: Recruit people who have the skill, or Train people to build it

Planning in the Real World

  • Seasonal scaling: e-commerce/logistics hiring surges before festive season
  • IT services "bench" model: trained staff kept ready for new client contracts

Recruitment vs. Selection

  • Recruitment = attracting candidates into the process
  • Selection = evaluating and choosing among them
  • 10,000 applicants → attracted (recruitment); 30 offers → chosen (selection)

Internal vs. External Recruitment

  • Internal: promoting existing employees (already know culture & systems)
  • External: careers websites, job portals, campus recruitment, referrals, agencies
  • Campus recruitment = most relevant channel for us

The Recruitment Funnel (DataTech Example)

  • Every stage generates data
  • Different sources can have very different success rates → this is where analytics begins

Real Company: Amazon (BIE Hiring)

  • Application → online assessment → (technical phone screen) → interview loop
  • Loop tests: SQL & scripting, analytical problem-solving, visualization, metrics/reporting, business requirements
  • Lesson: the job defines the required skill; the skill defines the hiring test

Real Company: Google (People Operations)

  • HR at Google = "People Operations," guided by a People Analytics team
  • Goal: bring engineering-level rigor to people decisions
  • Data-driven hiring: found 4 interviews capture most predictive value
  • "Project Janus": re-checked rejected resumes for missed strong candidates

Real Company: JPMorganChase

  • Four-stage journey: Explore → Apply → Interview → Decision
  • Process varies by role — some roles use standardized/virtual assessments
  • Same theme: process design follows job requirements

Careers Inside HR (Where BI Fits)

  • HR Business Partner — connects business goals to workforce decisions
  • Recruiter/Talent Acquisition — manages and optimizes the funnel
  • L&D Specialist — designs and measures training
  • People Analyst / People Analytics — builds dashboards & models on hiring, attrition, engagement (a real, hiring job title today)

Training vs. Development

  • Training = current-job oriented (e.g., learning the internal BI dashboard tool)
  • Development = future-oriented (e.g., leadership coaching for a future team-lead role)
  • Both close a gap — just different time horizons

The Training Process

  • Most orgs measure Reaction easily; Results rarely — a real measurement gap

Training & Development in the Real World

  • Amazon: large-scale upskilling investment for hundreds of thousands of employees
  • Retail/logistics "academy" programs preparing frontline staff for leadership roles
  • New system rollouts (ERP/BI tools) → structured certification training

Closing: Where BI Meets HR

  • Every stage — Planning, Recruitment, Training — generates data
  • Key metrics: time-to-hire, cost-per-hire, attrition rate, offer-acceptance rate, training ROI
  • Attrition prediction = same logic as customer-churn modeling, applied to employees
